**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Reviewer:** an independent review by a subagent, which used two helper subagents (app and pacing; explanations, heuristic and playbooks). The brief was to find real problems, not to confirm the builder's report. I re-checked every severity 3 and 4 finding in code myself.

**Build reviewed:**

- Checks ran on `origin/main` at `49c04e2`, in a clean worktree with its own `npm ci`, so `@optcg/*` resolves to the worktree's packages. E2E ran on its own port.
- No repository code was changed. Probe tests were written in the worktree and not committed; see Method.

**Rule (as in M4a):** any severity 3 or 4 finding fails the milestone.
- 4 = a wrong game result, or a leak of hidden information;
- 3 = a rules error that changes play, or a defect that blocks a task;
- 2 = should fix;
- 1 = nit.

M5a review: **FAIL** (open: M5a-01 at severity 4; M5a-02 and M5a-03 at severity 3).

The strength claim holds up, and the AI's own decisions are blind to hidden cards on every reachable pair I could build. The failures:
- **A hidden-information leak in the app.** Whether the AI's move gets a "Why?" chip depends on its private option count. The clearest case is whether the Life card it just took had a [Trigger].
- **A latent leak in the engine.** `projectForSearch` puts a non-viewer's decision into the skeleton verbatim. That decision can carry hidden card names and counts. The suite's leak property cannot see this class of leak.
- **An accessibility regression.** A screen reader never hears the result of an AI effect pick or of the later DON!! of a group.

Below those come a cluster of explanation-honesty issues (severity 2). The flagship one: a move won by the playbook bias is explained by a heuristic term that preferred the other move.

### The differential test (hidden information)

**Why a new test.** The suite's leak property, and Pirate's invariance test, build their "other" state by permuting the `card` field of hidden instances *after the fact*. Anything the engine already derived from those cards stays as it was:
- a decision prompt;
- option labels;
- the candidate list of a filtered choice.

So those tests cannot detect a leak through derived data.

**What my probe does instead:**
- It builds **two reachable games** from scenarios that differ only in cards hidden from viewer `v`. In "all" mode it reshuffles the opponent's hand, Life and deck, and `v`'s own deck and Life. In "life" mode it reverses only the opponent's Life.
- It replays the same random legal actions in both games.
- At every step where `project(A, v)` equals `project(B, v)`, so that the public information is identical, it requires `projectForSearch(A, v)` to equal `projectForSearch(B, v)`.

**Result:**
- **"All" mode, 300 seeds, 1,599 equal-view states:** 15 differences. They are in `decision.options[].label` (an order decision naming the opponent's looked-at deck cards) and in `decision.candidates` (a hand-trash cost whose candidate count differs).
- **"Life" mode, 300 seeds, 39,080 equal-view states:** 816 differences. They are in `decision.prompt` (`"Damage: activate the [Trigger] of Rocks Pirates, or add it to your hand?"` against `"…of Don Marlon…"`) and in `decision.candidates`.
- **Every difference is at a decision owed by the other player.** Restricted to the viewer's own decisions (600 seeds, both modes), I found no difference.

**The same pairs with Pirate deciding.** I ran the reachable-pair method with Pirate sessions in lockstep (40 seeds, 1,307 AI decisions, iteration budgets, the same seeds). Action and explanation were identical every time. Over 3,021 Pirate decisions with a choice in 16 Pirate-vs-Pirate games, no skeleton was opaque.

## Notes on severity

- **M5a-01 is a 4.** It leaks a fact about a hidden card on every AI damage window, in the default mode, through a control the player is invited to use. The engine was built (M2-01) so the opponent cannot tell a [Trigger] Life card from another; the chip undoes that. The counter case leaks through W.
- **M5a-02 is a 3, not a 4,** only because nothing sends a non-own skeleton to the AI today. I checked both callers and ran the differential test at own decisions: no difference. It is a broken contract on the one engine function whose purpose is to be handed to the AI, and the suite's own test claims to cover it but structurally cannot. It becomes a 4 the day `analyze` or a hint calls it at the human's decision.
- **M5a-03 is a 3** for the same reason M4a-05 was: for a screen-reader user, AI effects (discards, the −2000 target, DON!! counts) are missing or wrong.
- **The explanation findings (M5a-04 to M5a-07) are 2s** because no hidden card is named; the D9 probe found none over 1,276 decisions. But together they mean "Why?" sometimes teaches a reason the AI did not use, a result that did not happen, or a plan step not yet taken. For a learning tool, fix them before M5b builds on the templates.

The owner should know the PLAN exit line "AI turn ≤ 8 s at 1×" is met only for turns that board.md §5.2 calls typical (up to 6 moves; 3.5–7.5 s measured); the 15-move late-game turn takes 10.0 s.
